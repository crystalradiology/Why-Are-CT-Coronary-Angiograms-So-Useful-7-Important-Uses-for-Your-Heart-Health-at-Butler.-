Heart health is important at every stage of life. However, coronary artery disease can develop gradually, sometimes without obvious warning signs. When plaque builds up inside the arteries supplying blood to the heart, it can reduce blood flow and increase the risk of serious cardiovascular problems.
A CT coronary angiogram is an advanced, non-invasive imaging examination that allows doctors to examine the coronary arteries in detail. It can help identify narrowed or blocked arteries, assess plaque buildup, and investigate possible causes of chest discomfort.
Understanding the CT Coronary Angiogram Uses at Butler can help patients learn why their doctor may recommend this examination and how it supports the assessment of heart health.
7 Important Uses of CT Coronary Angiograms
There are several reasons why a doctor may recommend this examination. The following are some of the key CT Coronary Angiography uses at Butler.
1. Detecting Coronary Artery Disease
One of the main uses of CT coronary angiography is to investigate coronary artery disease (CAD).
CAD develops when plaque, made up of cholesterol, fatty substances, and other materials, accumulates inside the coronary artery walls. This can cause the arteries to become narrowed and restrict blood flow to the heart.
A CT coronary angiogram can help:
•	Identify plaque buildup within the coronary arteries.
•	Detect areas where the arteries have become narrowed.
•	Assess the extent and location of coronary artery disease.
•	Help doctors determine whether further investigation or treatment is needed.
Identifying coronary artery disease can help healthcare professionals develop an appropriate management plan.
2. Investigating Unexplained Chest Pain
Chest pain can occur for many reasons, including muscle problems, digestive conditions, and heart-related issues.
When a doctor suspects that chest discomfort may be related to coronary artery disease, CTCA may be recommended to examine the heart's blood vessels.
The scan can help investigate symptoms such as:
•	Chest pressure or tightness.
•	Discomfort that occurs during physical activity.
•	Chest pain that spreads towards the arms, neck, jaw, or back.
•	Unexplained discomfort that requires assessment for coronary artery disease.
CTCA can help doctors determine whether plaque or narrowing in the coronary arteries may explain the symptoms.
3. Identifying Narrowed or Blocked Coronary Arteries
Another important use of CTCA is assessing narrowing within the coronary arteries.
Plaque buildup can reduce the space available for blood to flow through an artery. In some cases, this may affect the amount of oxygen reaching the heart muscle.
CT coronary angiography produces detailed images that can help doctors:
•	Locate narrowed sections of coronary arteries.
•	Assess the severity and extent of narrowing.
•	Identify areas where blood flow may be affected.
•	Decide whether additional cardiac tests or specialist assessment may be appropriate.
The scan provides anatomical information about the arteries. It does not directly measure blood flow or prove whether a particular narrowing is causing symptoms.
4. Assessing Plaque Buildup in the Heart Arteries
Plaque is one of the main contributors to coronary artery disease. It can develop over many years and may be present even before a person experiences noticeable symptoms.
CTCA can identify different types of coronary plaque, including:
•	Calcified plaque: Plaque containing calcium deposits.
•	Non-calcified plaque: Plaque that does not contain visible calcium.
•	Mixed plaque: Plaque containing both calcified and non-calcified components.
This information helps doctors understand the nature and extent of coronary artery disease.
Unlike a coronary calcium score, which measures calcified plaque only, CT coronary angiography can assess both calcified and non-calcified plaque and show the coronary artery lumen.
5. Investigating Breathlessness and Other Possible Heart-Related Symptoms
Breathlessness during physical activity can sometimes be associated with reduced blood supply to the heart. However, it can also result from lung conditions, anaemia, and other medical problems.
When a doctor suspects a coronary artery problem, CTCA may help investigate whether narrowing or plaque in the heart arteries could be contributing to the symptoms.
Your doctor may consider this scan if you experience:
•	Unexplained breathlessness during exercise.
•	Reduced exercise tolerance.
•	Discomfort associated with physical activity.
•	Symptoms that may suggest an underlying coronary artery condition.
The results must be interpreted alongside your symptoms and other clinical findings.
6. Evaluating Patients with Risk Factors for Heart Disease
Some people have a higher risk of developing coronary artery disease because of their medical history or lifestyle factors.
Your doctor may consider CTCA when investigating possible coronary artery disease in patients with risk factors such as:
•	High cholesterol.
•	High blood pressure.
•	Diabetes.
•	Smoking history.
•	Family history of early heart disease.
•	Obesity or physical inactivity.
CTCA is not automatically recommended for everyone with these risk factors. Your doctor will assess your symptoms, overall risk, and whether the scan is appropriate.
7. Supporting Treatment and Further Medical Decisions
CT coronary angiography provides detailed information that can help doctors decide on the next steps in managing coronary artery disease.
Depending on the findings, your doctor may recommend:
•	Lifestyle changes, including improved nutrition and regular physical activity.
•	Medication to manage cholesterol or blood pressure.
•	Further cardiac investigations.
•	Referral to a cardiologist.
•	Assessment for an invasive coronary angiogram or other treatment when clinically necessary.
The scan can therefore play an important role in helping healthcare professionals understand coronary artery structure and plan appropriate care.
